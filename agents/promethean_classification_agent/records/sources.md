# Evidence checked for the CRISPR worked example

Checked against the original pages on 2026-10-18. These sources support technology and application facts; the organizations in the cases are hypothetical. A source about one product or research setting must not be generalized to every CRISPR application.

| ID | Original source and date | Supported claim and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Jinek et al., *Science*, 2012-06-28, [DOI 10.1126/science.1225829](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225829) | Demonstrates programmable RNA-guided Cas9 DNA cleavage. Mechanism, not scaled societal impact. |
| S2 | US FDA, [Casgevy approval announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapies-treat-patients-sickle-cell-disease), 2023-12-08, and [supplemental approval announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-young-children-sickle-cell-disease), 2026-07-01 | FDA approval demonstrates one clinical translation, with expanded pediatric indication in 2026. Approval is not evidence that all CRISPR treatments are safe, effective, or locally feasible. |
| S3 | Frangoul et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2024-04-24, [DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2309676](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2309676) | In a single-group phase 3 study, 29 of 30 evaluable recipients were free of severe vaso-occlusive crises for at least 12 consecutive months. Selection, sample size, limited follow-up and nonrandomized design bound the inference. |
| S4 | US FDA, [Casgevy prescribing information](https://www.fda.gov/media/174615/download), revised 2026-03 | Requires full myeloablative conditioning and warns that off-target editing risk cannot be ruled out. The March label predates the July pediatric expansion; use the July announcement for the current age indication. |
| S5 | Liu et al., *Nature Communications*, 2025-07-16, [DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-61943-3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61943-3) | CRISPR knockout experiments helped establish a wheat drought-tolerance gene's function. The paper also reports field results for other lines/alleles, but does not demonstrate a commercial CRISPR-edited wheat variety or broad farm impact. |
| S6 | World Health Organization, [Human genome editing governance framework](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240030060), 2021-07-12 | Supports the need for governance, oversight and attention to ethical and societal risks. It is guidance, not empirical proof of eventual civilization-scale benefit. |

## Evidence for and against the highest plausible classification

**For potentially Promethean:** S1 demonstrates a reusable programmable mechanism. S2-S3 show clinical translation; S5 shows use in crop research. Health and food systems are materially different domains. The possible system-level consequences are an inference, not an observed global outcome.

**Against a strong Promethean claim:** S3 is a bounded single-group clinical study; S4 documents substantial treatment burdens and unresolved off-target risk; S5 establishes a research finding rather than commercial agricultural impact. S6 adds governance concerns. Cross-domain use does not yet demonstrate broad, affordable, equitable, durable adoption or civilization-scale change.
